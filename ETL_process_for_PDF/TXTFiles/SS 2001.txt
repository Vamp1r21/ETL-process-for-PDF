ANOYAS already has a substantial reference list
 covering bulk carriers in the 46,000/48,000dwt range, and has recognized a further demand for vessels of this, so called, Handymax size. Agios Anastasios is one of a series ordered by Levant Maritime to a recently developed design, which offers increased hold capacity and deadweight, together with improved ptopulsive performance and, consequently, better fuel consumption, when compared with many other similar vessels, 
Built with a Panamax beam, although considerably shorter than is the norm for most bulkers of this type- designation, this new class has a capacity on scantlin, draught of 52,000dwt. A conventional single-skin hu configuration has been adopted, with top and bottom wing ballast tanks enhancing _ self-trimming characteristics, and assisting with grab discharge of coal and other bulk cargoes. Four grabs are included in the equipment, stowed alongside the cranes when not in use.

Agios Anastasios is self-sustaining with her five holds, setved by an outfit of Tsuji electric-hydraulic deck ctanes. This is comprised of three single units which provide coverage over all holds, plus a twin-crane positioned between Nos 3 and 4 holds. The latter gives additional capacity in either single or twin mode at these midship compartments.

As single units, each crane lifts 30tonnes at 25m/min (without grabs), whilst working as a pair, 60tonnes can be handled. This arrangement allows heavy cargoes such as hot-rolled steel coils and pipes to be loaded, making use of hatches more than 20m long, closed by
MacGregor-Kayaba weathertight folding steel covers that stow in pairs at each end of the hatch. The design also caters for the carriage of dangerous cargoes, and the holds are fitted with a mechanical ventilation system, CO: fire extinguishing, and smoke-tube fire
 etection.

The main engine is a Diesel United-Sulzer 6RTA48T-
B unit developing 8738kW at 127rev/min, driving a 5900mm diameter FP propeller. Allowing a 15% sea margin and operating at 85% MCR, service speed is 14.8knots. Three Daihatsu/Nishishiba 650kW alternators are fitted, and a composite boiler satisfies steam requirements. The installation complies with the
American Bureau of Shipping ACCU unmanned engineroom notation. Total complement is 25, accommodated in single cabins in a six-tier deckhouse.

HE fruitful combination of Polish shipyards
 building for German owners specialising in the liner- charter trades continues with this new B-178 series from
Szczecinska. This extends a successful range of similar designs from the company, some already featured in
Significant Ships reports, for example, CCNI Axgo/ in our 1998 edition. APL Venezuela is the first of 15 vessels, and with her two immediate sisters will operate on a charter which does not require cargo gear. Nevertheless, other ships in the class will be fitted with cranes, and a facility to retrofit this earlier trio with three slim cranes - each rated at 45tonnes swl at 27.5m outreach, or 40tonnes at 30.5m, positioned between holds 1/2; 3/4 and 5/6 - is built into the structure. Tank testing was cartired out at
CTO, Gdansk.

The B-178 is designed with excessive freeboard and has a double-skin hull containing six cargo holds fitted with cell guides suitable for 20/40ft containers, with 45/49ft units also carried on deck. No 1 hold is accessed through a long forecastle partly covered by a shelter/breakwater, and is arranged to carry dangerous catgoes of class 1.1 to 1.5, with classes 1.4, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 allowed in holds
Nos 2, 3, 4 and 5.

Of particular note are the stoppets fitted to the cell guides 5.25m above the tank top in holds 2 to 5, which allow break-bulk cargo to be loaded below 40ft containers, and the large refrigerated container capacity available: 300 units on deck and 200 in holds 2, 3, 4 and 5.

A tweendeck is arranged in the side tanks P&S providing pipe/cable ducts and access passages. At the aft end, this forms a full deck with store rooms, hobby room, and accommodation for a Suez Canal crew. The engineroom is positioned forward of the eight-tier supetstructure (which is built with a void between level 7 and the wheelhouse in order to give visibility over the containers); this leaves free deck over the main engine for maintenance access and additional cargo space. A notable feature is the inclusion of large back-stay brackets aft of the very short super structure to limit vibrations.

The propulsion unit is an MAN B&W 7K80MC-C unit built by Polish licensee H Cegielski and rated at 26,270kW when running at 104rev/min. Operating at 87.5% MCR, it gives a service speed of 22.04knots, Four
Cegielski-MAN B&W/van Kaick diesel-alternator sets

WO years ago Van der Giessen delivered the cable
 layer Bold Endeavour to Global Marine - formerly
Cable & Wireless - (Significant Ships of 1999) basing the design on that of an offshore supply vessel in its portfolio. For this second vessel - primarily required for rapid response cable maintenance and repait work, ROV operation, buoy deployment, and retrieval in water depths up to 5000m, within the areas covered by the Atlantic Cable Maintenance Agreement - it has moved away from the OSV configuration to introduce a flush-decked layout, surmounted by an almost symmetrical centre castle measuting three quarters of the length of the ship, evenly spread amidships.

The now standard ovet-the-stern working concept is retained, however, utilising two 3m diameter cable laying/repair sheaves, and a plough towing sheave, supported by a 10tonne 'A-ftame' for buoy handling, and cranage for ROV operation. The main working area is on deck No 4, covered by the centre castle and equipped with two 4m diamater/28tonne cable-drum engines and two, four-wheel-pair draw off/hold back engines and diverters. Twin two-wheel-pair cable transporters ate also installed, with all the cable handling machinery (which was supplied by Dowty) operated by built-on hydraulic power packs.

Two gantry crane systems serve an atea of 950m’, with another 400m’ of open space available aft where 2 x 10tonne tugger winches are fitted. Storerooms and workshops are provided at the sides of deck 4 and the main deck (No 3), below which there are two 425m? cable tanks and two 110m’ spare tanks. A cable equipment hold (600m) is arranged in the stern with additional storage in a 300m’ hold forward. Uniform loading on the open work deck is 5tonnes/m’.

Atlantic Guardian has been designed for a high transit speed, and an ability to keep position in sea state 6 with

 a 30knot side wind, and 1knot current coincident on the beam. Thus a dynamic positioning system including sensors, reference systems complying with the DP(AA) notation, portable control unit, taut wire, transducers, stabilised bottom profiler, and motion reference unit, has been installed. Operating modes provided are standby, manual/joystick, mixed auto/manual, auto heading and track, automatic station keeping, follow target, and cable tension control via computerised cable machinery.

Contributing to the DP system are two Lips L-type independently controllable azimuthing propulsion thrusters with CP propellers, operating in high efficiency nozzles fitted under a ptam-stern, and three constant speed CP lateral bow thrusters. All are supplied from a high voltage diesel-electric power installation using three ABB 2690kW alternators driven by Wartsila 9L26A diesel engines. A Flume passive tank stabilising system is fitted to reduce roll.

 'HIS is the third oil/chemical tanker to be built for
Petromatine by the Niestern shipyard (which was awatded the prestigious title ‘Royal’ this year on reaching its centenary), and another larger capacity vessel is currently under construction. With a beam restriction of 16.00m in the Eemskanaal, Bedisaire was built in pre-outfitted sections at the shipyard, and these were then taken downstream by barge for assembly in a floating dock outside the Delfzijl lock system. Since 1996, this has been the yard's standard procedure, so that it can compete for contracts involving 'wide' ships.
The vessel is a double-hull oil and chemical tanker, designed to carry IMO Type II products with a flash point below 60 C in six pairs of centrally divided cargo tanks. With side and bottom structure contained within the double hull, external upper deck stiffening and troughed longitudinal and transverse bulkheads, the tanks have flush interiors for optimum cleaning, 
A 300m’/h Framo SD150 deepwell pump is fitted in each tank, giving a maximum discharge rate of 1800m°/h with six pumps running, and filling is accomplished at 800m°/h for each tank pair. Six cargo segregations can be handled through a piping system executed in 316L stainless steel and utilising remotely operated valves. The cargo pumps include a stripping system with the operation enhanced by a 100mm slope to the centreline of the tank bottom.

Cargo is heated to 65°C by means of secondary thermal oil in a stainless steel deck heater at each tank, and tank washing uses two fixed Toftejorg tank washing machines in each tank. Two portable machines ate also available. A Smit Gas nitrogen generator in the engineroom can supply 825m°/h to a storage tank on deck for use when cargo-padding or pipeline Posging

Facilities available to each cargo tank, and the 398m? slop tank, include tadar-type sounding, and temperature and pressure sensing, with high-level alarms at 95% and 98% capacity. All sensors are connected to a monitoring computer in the cargo control room. A 5tonne swl hydraulic hose-handling crane is positioned at the manifold and_ three
Yokohama-type fenders are stowed on deck within its

The main engine is an MaK 8M32C developing 3840kW at 600rev/min full power. A Vulkan Rato coupling connects with a Metso (Valmet) reduction gearbox with a PTO to a 750kW/1800rev/min alternator. This can be used 'in reverse’ as a drive motor for emergency tT ae when the main engine clutch is disconnected. This allows a service speed of around 7knots to be attained. A high-efficiency flap-type rudder and a 500kW bow thruster are also fitted.

 extended its portfolio of specialised shuttle tanker designs to include at least seven vessels intended principally for service in North Sea/North Atlantic waters. The newest, Bertora, is built to the requirements of Norwegian operator Navion AS, and to a Norske
Veritas classification. This includes references to the
NAUTICUS (newbuilding) and CSA-1(25) notations, as well as covering this classification society's dynamic position DYNPOS-AUTR requirements, and the equivalent IMO DP Class 2 standard.

Bertora is a single-deck vessel, based on Samsung's conventional Aframax tanker design, with a double-hull structure conforming to the OPP-F notation. The catgo space contains six pairs of centrally divided tanks, surrounded by wing tanks utilised for water ballast, which incorporate a hopper configuration at the tank top, where they are joined with double-bottom tanks separated by the centre girder. Structural and other arrangements have been made for the future fitting of a plant to deal with VOCs (volatile organic compounds), and related power generation.

Cargo handling is arranged for two segregations, using GRP piping and four 2000m°/h electrically driven, Hamworthy KSE pumps, which can be operated at variable speed by means of speed changes at the diesel-alternators. Loading is accomplished over the bow through a Hitec loading/mooring system that allows the vessel to be safely connected to the offshore hose with minimum risk of an oil spill.

A vital component of this operation is the fully redundant Kongsberg Simrad dynamic positioning installation. This works in combination with an integrated bridge system plus navigation and communication equipment, supplied by the Norcontrol division of Kongsberg, for station-keeping and maintaining continuous loading in sea states up to 5.5m significant wave heights.

Assisting with manoeuvring and station-keeping requirements are a pait of Brunvoll tunnel-type thrusters (2200kW forward and 1400kW aft), and a retractable azimuthing unit of 2200kW at the bow. Also contributing to these operations ate a CP propeller and a Schilling rudder. A feature of the design is the inclusion of emergency towing arrangements at the aft end of the hull.

Korean combine HSD Diesel has supplied the MAN
B&W 7S60MC main engine which develops 19,460bhp (MCR) at 105rev/min and which can sustain a service speed of 14.5knots at the design draught when operating at an NCR of 17,510bhp (90%MCR) and allowing a sea margin of 15%. Electrical requirements ate served by four Daihatsu/Nishishiba 2400kW diesel- alternator sets, with steam provided by an auxiliary, and a composite boiler, rated at 25tonnes/h and 1.2tonnes/h respectively.

The design is single-decked with forecastle, and features a double hull. This contains a cargo space divided into six pairs of tanks by troughed longitudinal and transvetse bulkheads built on stools. The double bottom under Nos 1, 2 and 6 cargo tanks is centrally divided into pairs of water ballast tanks common with the adjacent side tanks, but in Nos 3, 4 and 5 tanks, longitudinal girders form three tanks: port, starboard, and centre, with the outer compartments joined the side tanks.

The fore and aft, and athwartship bulkhead stools above the centre tanks, are arranged as void spaces. The tank side-stiffening is contained within the double-hull space and the structure of the cambered upper deck is external. This, in association with the troughed bulkhead arrangement, means the cargo tanks present a flush internal surface. The latter is coated with a pure epoxy composition suitable for the carriage of oil products and IMO Type IIT chemicals, notably caustic soda.

Dangerous goods of categories 1 to 8 can be carried in No 1 hold, and 2 to 8 in holds 2/3/4.
To complete their versatility, the vessels can accommodate six passengers. Othets in the series, to be completed next year, will have even higher-capacity cranes.

The double-skin hull has five holds, with Nos 2 to 5 divided by a centreline bulkhead, also of double-skin construction. No 1 hold is accessed through a long forecastle and is built over a deep double bottom. Holds 2/4 are box-shaped and can sustain a uniform tank top loading of 22tonnes/m*. They are strengthened for gtabs and can carry two layers of 25tonne steel coils.

Holds 1 and 5, are fitted with cell guides for 20ft containers, with a similar arrangement serving the deck space above the engineroom in front of the deckhouse, where stack loading is 90/110tonnes for 20ft and 40ft containers, respectively. HAM 378 is the new flagship of a fleet operated by Anglo-Dutch dredging contractor HAM and has a hopper capacity of 23,700m* and a corresponding deadweight of 40,560tonnes. It is interesting to note that compared with other, so called, 'jumbo-dredgers' featured in recent Significant Ships reviews, this vessel is laid out with accommodation and propulsion machinery positioned aft. This arrangement allows the two (port and starboard) 1200mm diameter trailing suction pipes to be operated from a position forward of midships to a maximum dredging depth of 70m.

Each pipe, which weighs 360tonnes and is handled by three hydraulic gantries and three electric winches, uses an 8m-wide draghead weighing 50tonnes which can be controlled hydraulically to suit local circumstances, and is fitted with a 2500kW submerged dredge pump, with two more dredge pumps installed on board. The latter operate at 2750kW whilst the vessel is trailing, and 5500kW when discharging ashore. Facilities have been included for adapting the starboard trailing pipe to teach a depth of 110m in the future.

During both trailing and discharging, the pumping process is supported by jet water nozzles fed by two 2150kW jet pumps. All the pumps have been supplied by IHC. As HAM 378 will be required to operate in areas without supporting facilities, workshop space and spares storage has been provided, and a 60tonne gantry crane covers the entire length of the ship.

Te innovative methods adopted by shipowners and shippers in order to preserve the quality of forest products cargoes have been described in a number of Significant Ship reviews over the years, but few have offered so novel a solution to the problem as
Jaeger Arrow. Her design is made even mote interesting by the inclusion of a facility to also carry sizeable cargoes of liquid bitumen (coal-tar pitch).

Delivered into the fleet of Gearbulk, one of the world's largest bulk-cargo carriers, Jaeger Arrow is basically of double-hull configuration with poop and forecastle, and a cargo space divided into five holds and four bitumen tanks: two (one port and one starboard) forward of No 1 hold and two in a similar arrangement aft of No 5. Uniquely covering all but the extreme ends of the vessel is a complete superstructure, which is further extended at the bow by a 'whaleback' shelter built over the forward mooring position. Above this structure is a  single-/double-tier deckhouse, accommodating a complement of 31, and surmounted by a midships wheelhouse with all-round vision.

The purpose of the superstructure is to afford completely dry working of cargo in the five hatchcoverless holds, for which purpose two Tsuji overhead gantry cranes are fitted under this structure, stowing aft when not in use, and travelling over most of the ship's length. They operate in conjunction with a pait of 17m wide sideports arranged at midships on the starboard side only. Also designed by Tsuji, these hinge upwards to act as rain shelters, with closure completed by curtains on three sides.

A telescopic arm on each crane has an outteach of 7.5m through the sideports, and carries trolleys with a 36tonne lift for unitised cargoes such as paper rolls, pulp bales, steel coils, packaged lumber, and aluminium ingots. The system can work at a continuous rate of 25cycles/h against a list of 5deg and 2deg trim, but an anti-heeling system is included in the specification.

As a secondary cargo, Jaeger Arrow can carry 7450m°> of bitumen in the four independent tanks, which are specially supported to counteract thermal expansion, and insulated to prevent temperature drops exceeding 1°C/24h. Tank heating is through mild steel double- loop coils that can maintain the cargo at 220°C in a sea temperature of 14°C and an ambient of -35°C. Cargo handling is by means of four Leistritz screw pumps.

The machinery installation is based on a Hyundai-
MAN B&W 6SGOMC-C main engine developing 17,660bhp at 101rev/min, directly coupled to a FP propeller for a service speed of 18knots. The three
 58

Tee dynamically positioned vessel has been designed with worldwide cable laying and maintenance in mind, and is laid out with normal ship operations, the machinery space, and _ the accommodation block concentrated right forward, leaving the large after deck free for a fully stern- working concept. The hull is largely of double-skin construction, with a pram-shaped after body featuring a central skeg.

Three main cable tanks and three smaller, spare tanks ate fitted in the midship body below the cable deck.
These have a total capacity of 5700m°* for 12mm to 150mm diameter cable, which is drawn by port and starboard cable-drum engines along the two cable lines to the stern sheaves, by way of three draw-off/hold- back units, and two transporter winches.

Most of the cable working equipment, together with jointing and testing rooms, is positioned in a superstructute which extends from the bow for %4L over the cable deck. Stowage is arranged on the (open) aft end of the cable deck for a remote-operated vehicle (ROV) which can be handled over the stern by a 60tonne capacity A-frame. A 20tonne knuckle-boom crane and a similar 10tonne unit serve the working spaces, including handling and stowage of the sea plough.

The machinery installation has been designed within the parameters of a 15.42knot transit speed, a cable laying speed of 6knots-8knots and a ploughing speed of 4knots, derived from two fully azimuthing 4500kW nozzled thrusters fitted aft. These also form part of a
Kongsberg-Simrad dynamic positioning system with
HPR, taut-wite, DGPS and MRU reference units, working in conjunction with two retractable 2000kW azimuth thrusters and a 1320kW tunnel thruster in the bow. The system satisfies DNV requirement ERN 98 and enables the vessel to lay and repair cable in rough sea conditions, significant wave height 5.6m, wind speed 19.9m/sec, and current condition 0.775m/sec.

Power for propulsion, DP installation, ROV, sea plough, cable operations, and all hotel services comes tom four diesel-alternator sets, each producing 3705kW. Transit speed is expected to be maintained with only three sets working in parallel at 90% rating, and allowing a 15% sea margin. The DP/precision tracking system, propulsion machinery and bow thruster control, alarm and monitoring systems, 60
 navigation and data integration systems, cable control, and measuting systems are integrated so that cable and vessel position can be plotted on a bottom profiler output overlaid on the navigation chatt.

Navigation and cable-laying operations are carried out from a large wheelhouse/control room, positioned over the four-tier accommodation block designed for a complement of 77 persons. Additional claims made specifically for the SSP design include: less resistance due to elimination of an external headbox; reduced headroom required for installation and maintenance; also a flush, obstruction- free car deck since the motors are cooled by seawater and do not need space to accommodate overhead fans.

Unlike other self-unloading vessels described in

Sagittarius uses a simpler grabbing arrangement as described below.

Built to double-skin format with wide side tanks utilised for water ballast, the hull is divided into six, largely box-shaped cargo holds, by transverse double- skin bulkheads. No 3 hold is floodable for use as a water ballast tank. The hold sides are vertical with a slight slope at the bottom, allowing approximately 98% of the cargo to be discharged mechanically.

The single deck is flush and fitted with large pontoon hatch covers at each hold, giving almost unobstructed access to the cargo space. The covers stow on top of each other in a ‘piggy-back' arrangement, moved by means of the travelling gantry cranes which form the main components of the self-unloading equipment; these run on fore and aft tracks laid at the hatch sides, P&S.

Two gantry cranes are fitted; these are capable of travelling at 30m/min and each carries an eight-rope 49tonne/30m? shell-type grab bucket, which can traverse in an athwartship direction, also a wide hopper and a feeder conveyor. The width of the grab bucket is approximately one-third hold width, and in the fully automated and computet-controlled, discharge sequence, the gantry makes three runs along the deck.

This allows the grab to lift cargo from the port, centre, and starboard sections of the holds in turn, and deposit it in the hopper. The hopper includes a sensor for coal height and coal depth, thus allowing fully automatic operation.

Three cargo manifolds on deck allow for three cargo segregations and ate positioned so that their centre above the waterline meets a maximum ait-draught of 22.50m in a normal harbour ballast departure condition.

The piping system can also be arranged to load two parcel cargoes in approximately 30/70; 40/60; or 50/50 proportions. Ballast handling is by means of two 3000m*/h electric pumps.

Usk is a single-decked vessel without forecastle but with a sunken aft deck, on which the funnel casing is built. Forward of that on the upper deck, a five-tier deckhouse, surmounted by the navigating bridge and wheelhouse, accommodates a crew of 32 and a six- petson repair crew.

The machinery installation is centred upon a Hanjun- built MAN B&W 7S80MC (Mk6) main engine developing 34,650bhp at 79rev/min MCR. When operating at an NCR of 31,190bhp/776.3rev/min, a service speed of 15.3knots is achieved, allowing a 15% sea margin.
