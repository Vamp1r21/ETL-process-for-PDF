CCORDING to the Maritime Administration,
malienborg is the first ocean-going vessel to be built
in the USA fot export for 40 years (the other contender
for that honour, the first Eletson tanker from Newport
News - American Progress, Significant Ships of 1997 - was
sold to US operator Mobil prior to delivery). The design,
developed by Norwegian consultant Skipskonsulent,
conforms to the single-deck/double-hull layout, with
troughed centreline and transverse bulkheads dividing
the cargo space into 12 tanks, two slop tanks and a small
cargo drain tank. With deck stiffening arranged exter-
nally, and tank-side structures contained within the dou-
ble skin, the tanks are plain sided for easy cleaning and
maintenance, and have been coated with Sigma Phen-
guard epoxy.

Cargo is handled through stainless steel piping using
Framo submerged pumps in each tank. The side tanks
incorporate sloped top and bottom wings, and together
with the double-bottom tanks (except two utilised for
technical water) ate designated for water ballast. Fuel is
carried in bunkers abreast the machinery space, separated
from the shell by cofferdams.

room and an engine control room are also provided.
Behind the deckhouse the separate funnel casing is off-

Nex. privately owned, the Barreras shipyard never-
theless still retains strong links with its former par-
ent, AESA, an established leader in the ferry industry,
as a result of a series of designs successfully imple-
menting the many regulations instituted in the wake of
the Herald of Free Enterprise, and other recent disasters.
Aratere, therefore, is a one-compartment-standard ves-
sel with a pedigree, complying with the requirements of
Stab90, including withstanding 500mm of water on the
main deck, and meeting the demands of the Stockholm
tules.

The Inter-islander service across the Cook Strait
between Wellington and Picton on which Aratere will
serve, covets 51 miles and includes 26 miles of open sea
subject to notoriously bad weather conditions. To
counter these, a pair of Brown Brothers fin stabilisers,
and an Intering air-activated heel control system which
uses anti-heeling tanks of 860m’ capacity, and includes
test equipment enabling stability conditions to be con-
firmed before sailing, are fitted.

The vessel can accommodate 355 day passengers,
plus 20 drivers carried in two-berth cabins with private
toilet facilities, and having their own mess and TV
lounge. For the remainder, there ate a seties of lounges,
a self-service cafeteria, tavern bar, cinema, TV and writ-
ing rooms, and outdoor seating,

Simultaneous access over shore-based linkspans is
atranged directly over the stern for rail wagons and
toad vehicles on to the train (main) deck, and for vehi-
cles only, to the partially enclosed vehicle deck above. A
tamp and door in the aft port quarter provides an alter-
native entry for vehicles to the train deck. A fixed ramp
links that deck with a lower hold having a capacity for
70 cars, and a hydraulically operated ramp connects the
train and vehicle decks.

Four recessed rail tracks provide 425 lane metres for
parking rail or road vehicles on the train deck, and 580
lane metres ate available for trucks on the vehicle
deck. All spaces (except the hold) can carry dangerous
cargoes other than Class 6.2 (infectious substances),
with the train deck suitable for A,B and E stowage cat-
egories, and the vehicle deck adding categories C and

8

D to its capability - all in accordance with IMDG
codes.

A diesel-electric machinery installation uses four
Wartsila 8L32 main engines driving ABB alternators to
supply power to an ABB SAMI Megastar plant with
four 2600kW propulsion motors geared to twin FP
propellers. All electrical requirements are served from
this system, including two Kamewa 1000kW bow
thrusters.

INCANTIERI moved into the commercial high-

speed ferry market as a natural development of its
naval work, introducing the steel-hulled Pegasus 7 in
1996 (Significant Ships of 1996), built to the MDV
(Monohull Deep Vee) 1200 design, where '1200' signi-
fies the approximate displacement. The Swperseacat
class - featured in the 1997 edition of this annual -
with aluminium alloy hull followed. Now the series is
continued by the first of four MDV 3000 Jupiter-class
vessels, designed to operate between Civitavecchia
(Rome) and Olbia in Sardinia, making up to six cross-
ings each day.

Built with a 145m-long steel hull and aluminium
superstructure, Aries is understood to be the largest car-
carrying fast ferry in the world. There are six decks:
three providing accommodation for 1800 passengers,
and three - lower, main and hoistable upper garage -
decks, with 2000 lane metres for 460 cars, or combina-
tions such as 30 trailers or coaches, plus 100 cars. Pas-
senger and vehicle access is through three ramp/doors
in the flat stern, at main garage deck level.

The former enter over a centreline ramp into an
enclosed vestibule with reversible escalators to the pas-
senger decks. Vehicles use the 9m long ramps at the side
entries, and these together with the wide beam, allow
traffic to perform a ‘U-turn! at the fore end to exit over
the stern, so dispensing with bow doors. Internal ramps
take vehicles to the lower garage, and to the upper
hoistable deck which is formed of 26 hydraulically
operated panels.

An alternative entry for passengers is at main deck
level, through side doors amidships, adjacent to MES
(marine evacuation system) positions capable of evac-
uating all on board within 15 minutes. This deck
accommodates 1208 tourist class, mainly seated in
saloons fitted with blocks of three/four seats. Public
spaces on this deck consist of a snack bar/lounge aft.
The upper passenger deck is for first-class travellers
with open seating areas, and 584 reclining seats within
saloons.

A number of alternative machinery installations are
available for the MDV3000 design. That selected for
Aries uses four MTU 20V 1163 TB73 main engines

10

rated at 6500kW each, connected to the largest steering
water jets yet built, through Maag twin-input/single-
output reduction gearboxes, plus two General Electric
22,000kW gas turbines which drive the two inboard
booster water jets, through two more sets of Maag
reduction gears.

This arrangement allows all six propulsive units to be
used for 40knot speeds; four diesel engines and one tur-
bine to give 31knots and four diesel engines alone provide
23knots. Four 820kW diesel-alternators are installed, and
there are two 600kW bow thrusters, and two pairs of
active computer-controlled fin stabilisers.

1S hearer has experienced a long run of success
with its design of circa-6000-unit car and truck
carriers, now constructed in a building dock dedicated
to this type of vessel, thus increasing productivity and
the final quality of the product. Classed with both Det
Norske Veritas and the Korean Register, Asian Captain
can carty a variety of motor vehicle types, loaded by
roll-on/roll-off methods, as well as containers brought
on board by trailer or container-carrying forklift trucks.

A total of 12 decks, including four which are
hoistable, as well as tank top and open top deck, are
available for vehicle transport, offering 53,750m? of
stowage space. Transverse racking bulkheads have been
dispensed with on No 5 (main/freeboard) deck, and
this has provided much improved efficiency of loading
and unloading, which is carried out over a stern quarter
ramp/door, and over a height-adjustable side door for-
ward of the engineroom on the main deck. Both these
entries are arranged on the starboard side.

Within the vessel, six moveable ramps link the main
deck with other decks, constructed so that they will be
flush with the surrounding level when closed. All the
access equipment has been supplied by Kvaerner and is
designed to operate within heeling angles up to 3deg,
and trim up to 2deg; audible and visual alarms indicat-
ing these angles are provided at the stern and side
ramps. A one-man control system is provided for each
of the items except the manual lashing stoppers. In the
cargo ateas, support is provided by two rows of pillars
installed at clear distances of approximately 13.4m in a
longitudinal direction, and 11.4m transversely.

Hyundai built the MAN B&W 7S60MC main engine
which develops 19,460bhp MCR at 105rev/min, and
three MAN B&W Holeby diesel-alternator sets each
produce 1180kW at 720rev/min, including supply to a
Kawasaki bow thruster with an output of 1800hp. A
Becker flap rudder is also fitted. The cargo space is sub-
divided into four groups for ventilation purposes, with

12

each receiving not less than 20 air changes hourly in
port. Maximum fume concentrations during cargo han-
dling comply with Australian Department of Health
Regulations 1975.

The hull is double-skinned up to deck 3, with the
resultant side tanks linked to the outboard double-bot-
tom tanks and used for water ballast. The central dou-
ble-bottom tanks are divided by the centre girder and
carry fuel. The design features a number of measures
aimed at easy operation and reducing maintenance.
Notable are the use of GRP piping for the main ballast
lines, a central cooling system for engineroom auxil-
iaries, and low-pressure hydraulics for deck machinery.

'LANSMAN is believed to be the first open-deck

has been increased in order to reduce the water-on-deck
penalty, and freeing port areas made larger than was the
case with earlier vessels. The imposition of these new
rules on to a design whose dimensions were already
restricted by conditions at the operating ports of Oban,
Castlebay, Lochboisdale, Coll and Tiree, has necessitated
a hullform of larger block co-efficient than might have
been expected.

The through vehicle deck is covered, except for an
area at the aft end, by an upper deck with tiered deck-
houses. Access is over an articulated stern ramp with
hinged finger and bridge flaps, and a bow ramp in three
sections, fitted behind a watertight bow visor which is
integral with the ship's structure. Up to 90 cars can be
cattied on the vehicle deck, with an extra 10 on a
hoistable mezzanine deck on the starboard side. All
equipment is hydraulically operated.

Wing buoyancy spaces are provided at each side of
the vehicle deck, and a central casing includes passenger
accesses to the accommodation. Below this deck are the
engineroom; main control room; compartment for the
twin Ulstein 450kW bow thrusters; port and starboard
stabilisers; fuel, water ballast and void spaces.

Accommodation is arranged for 634 passengers on
the upper and boat decks, with the main entrance con-
course amidships on the upper deck. The lounge bar is
aft, and a TV lounge (starboard), lounge with semi-
reclining chairs (port), and cafeteria with galleys and
servery, forward. An observation lounge with
panoramic views occupies the fore end of the boat
deck, and includes a number of fully reclining chairs for
overnight passengers. Crew members are housed aft of
this lounge, with officers one deck higher, behind a full-
width wheelhouse.

Th concentrated effort by Samsung to enter the
market for specialist offshore vessels has already
been rewarded with contracts for FPSO unit and shut-
tle tankers (such as Kometik, Significant Ships of 1997).
Today, this strategy continues with what are understood
to be the first ultra-deep-water drill ships, so described
because of theit designed ability to work at 2286m
water depths in the Gulf of Mexico, with a potential to
operate down to 3048m.

Deepwater Pathfinder has been built with a double hull
which complies with OPA-90 requirements, and is sub-
divided by two longitudinal bulkheads and transverse
divisions to form a series of holds/tanks suitable for
water ballast, oil, drilling water, mud, cement, barytes
and bentonite. Equipment is also provided for an
extended well test system (EW), including 100,000bb1
oil storage in which treatment of well fluid can be car-
tied out in environmentally sound conditions.
Simultaneous drilling and testing can also be main-
tained.

Also within the hull is a 12.8m x 12.48m moonpool,
through which the 52m tall derrick operates; port,
starboard and centreline compartments, forward and
aft (total six), for 4000kW ABB/Kamewa azimuthing
thrusters; a forward auxiliary machinery room; and
port, starboard and centre main enginerooms aft,
which house the diesel-electric power plant. The lat-
ter comprises 3 x 7000kW and 3 x 4700kW
Wartsila/ABB alternator sets (Geislinger elastic
damping couplings are fitted to each) supplying power
to the thrusters.

A high degree of redundancy has been incorporated

40

into the design, particularly with regard to the dynamic
positioning system, for which American Bureau of
Shipping's DPS-3 notation - indicating triple redundan-
cy - has been awarded to a drill ship for the first time.
Combined with satellite and seabed positioning sys-
tems, this ensures that the vessel can maintain station
and drill continuously in sea states up to 10-year Gulf
of Mexico storm level.

Hotel-standard accommodation for 130 persons is
provided in a ‘low-noise’ superstructure built on the
forecastle, and surmounted by a helicopter deck.
Extensive deck space available on the upper deck has
been utilised by houses containing workshops and
electrical equipment, with pipe racks above. Aft of
the derrick, stowage is arranged for risers, and the
open deck provides a location for rented well test
equipment and other gear. Three gantry-type cranes
of 25tonnes, 50tonnes and 320tonnes respectively,
serve the storage areas, and there are also three
pedestal cranes of 50tonnes SWL each, and one of
85tonnes.

HEN Disney Cruise Line ordered Disney Magic

and her sister Disney Wonder, the intention was to
bring back, as far as was possible, the traditional exter-
nal shape and elegance of the pre/post-war classic lin-
ers, and the accompanying photograph shows that this
has certainly been achieved. The entire concept demon-
strates typical Disney innovation. For example, it is pos-
sible for passengers to check their luggage in at their
local airport and next find it in their cabin on board ship
in America - and again waiting at Orlando Airport for
the flight home.

Originality continues throughout the seven-day vaca-
tion package offered by Disney. This involves a three-
ot four-day cruise from Port Canaveral, followed by a
four- or three-day stay at the Walt Disney World Resort
on the company’s private Bahamian island, Castaway
Cay. Arrangements, both on the island and aboard ship,
are based upon providing completely separate activities
for the categories of passenger carried: adults, families
and children, all fashioned with typical Disney flamboy-
ance and attention to detailed organisation.

One deck on Disney Magic is solely for children, pto-
viding an area of 1350m’ for their fully-supervised
entertainment. Teenagers have theit own club in one of
the funnels, with virtual reality and computer centres.
Adults have sole use of a pool, spa and fitness areas,
and within the forward funnel a multi-screen sports
club which broadcasts worldwide sports news and
events. They also have a separate restaurant for their
own use, but the main eating experience is provided
from three themed restaurants offering ‘rotational din-
ing’, where diners, table companions and waiters rotate
en-bloc each night between the three venues. Two the-
atres, one of which seats 975 and is three-decks high,
ate the other major entertainment centres for the 2400
maximum passengers normally carried in 879 cabins,
73% of which are ‘outside’, with 41% of that total hav-
ing a balcony.

Power supply is derived from a diesel-electric instal-
